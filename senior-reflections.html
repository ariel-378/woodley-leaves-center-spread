Senior Reflections

Waving Goodbye: Community Members Reflect on the Class of 2026

By Travis Riemer

As the school year comes to a close, our beloved seniors are preparing to head off to their bright futures. To celebrate the Class of 2026, the Woodley Leaves invited several community members, including seniors themselves, to reflect on what makes this grade special.

Ms. Krein, who has gotten to know the seniors through her role as both a college counselor and humanities teacher, identified key traits she has observed in the class. "Collectively and individually, the Class of 2026 exhibits intellectual curiosity, an eagerness to understand different perspectives, collaborative leadership of extracurriculars, and a wonderful balance between seriousness of purpose and spirited playfulness," she said.

Daphne Easterly-Zebley ('26) reflected on her time at 3000 Cathedral. "Maret has set me up for a lot of success academically and socially. It's given me a lot of unique opportunities and connections with teachers," she said. Declaring herself "satisfied with my time here," Easterly-Zebley added, "I feel ready to go out into the world."

Senior Henry Macauley says that he will miss Maret because it is a place where he has experienced "a lot of growth over the years." Looking back on his time in the pond, he reflected, "There have been a lot of highs and lows," and "I've seen a lot of faces come and go. I'm really thankful for all the peers, teachers, and faculty members that have helped me along the way."

For some seniors, activities outside the classroom have had great significance. For Jason Nguyen ('26), it was getting involved in theatre. "In my 11th grade year, I took the leap into the musical, and after my performance I stood there and saw everyone applaud my performance," he said. The moment turned out to be especially meaningful for him because "I saw the community here and realized I could be myself and have fun, and I wouldn't be judged for how good I was or how long I've done something. Everyone was just having fun and supporting each other."

When thinking about his time at Maret, Justin Qejvani ('26) mentioned sports as playing an important role. "One of my favorite memories was winning the state championship" when he was a sophomore, he explained.

It did not take long for the seniors to make an impression on Mr. Charles, who joined Maret this year as the Upper School Counselor and a Wellness teacher. "I have always appreciated knowing the things they are about to experience in their multiple colleges, and how that will shape them as young adults. I feel like it's so exciting." Mr. Charles has not found it difficult to connect with the seniors. "I vividly remember not only being a senior in high school but also being a senior in college, so I feel like I can relate to them," he explained.

As the seniors begin their next chapters, it can be hard to say goodbye. This class has brought a wide variety of unique interests, incredible talents, and personal connections to our community. Even after they leave campus, the Class of 2026's impact on Maret will still be strong. As Ms. Krein aptly noted, "We will miss their presence in classrooms, in the athletic arena, and as role models in our community." Once a frog, always a frog!
